Overview of the Course: 
Grade 11 IB Physics marks the start of a two-year course designed to deepen the understanding and application of 
physics concepts and principles. The course uses a student-centered, inquiry-based approach to explore various 
physical phenomena. Students will analyze and process data to uncover relationships between physical variables, 
leading to logical conclusions about these connections. Besides scientific investigation, students will engage in 
problem-solving activities that involve critical thinking and the basic application of physics formulas.

Summative Assessments – 60% 
Written Assessments – 30% 
Semester Exam – 10% 
 
Homework assignments will be given each time there is class. In most cases, students will have time to 
complete the homework in class. Answers to questions will be provided the following class with the intent 
that students will reflect on why answers were correct, partially correct, or incorrect. 
 
Laboratory experiments will often be done at the beginning of a unit. The goal is for students to make 
connections between quantities – specifically the independent and dependent variables under investigation. 
 
Formative assessments will be given every class and vary in nature. The goal is for students is to rehearse 
and reflect on their learning to improve on the learning outcome. 
 
Summative assessments will be given at the end of a unit. 
 
Class structure (students can expect the following): 
 
(1) Question and answer session on any homework assigned. 
(2) Engagement session – A brief five-minute introduction to a concept that motivates thinking about 
the phenomenon. 
(3) Exploration – A lab activity involving the collection of data from an online simulation or through 
the use of laboratory equipment. 
(4) Explanation – A ten to twenty minute explanation of the concepts and material under investigation. 
Students will be expected to take notes. Various types of formative assessments will be utilized to 
help students improve and reflect on the learning outcomes. 
(5) Elaboration – Students will be assigned conceptual questions or computational questions with 
regards to the physics principles under investigation.

Significant figure rules
● A non-zero digit is always significant: 789 is 3 s.f.
● A zero between two non-zero digits is always significant: 709 is 3 s.f.
● Leading zeros are never significant: 0.0123 is 3 s.f.
● Trailing zeros are not significant when there is no decimal point: 54800 is 3 s.f.
● Zeros to the left of a decimal point are significant: 54800. is 5 s.f.
● Zeros to the right of a decimal point and to the right of a non-zero digit are significant: 0.009 is 1 s.f; 0.023 is
2 s.f.; 0.175 is 3 s.f.; 0.17500 is 5 s.f.

\n--- PAGE 41 ---\nThe effect of fluid resistance on a projectile
(1) The range is decreased.
(2) The trajectory is no longer parabolic.
(3) The maximum height is reduced.
(4) The vertical acceleration is no longer constant.
(5) There is now a horizontal deceleration.
(6) Speeds are reduced.
(7) The determination of the time of flight becomes
complex and depends on the initial conditions of the
motion.

Sankey Diagrams The Sankey diagram is a _________________________ of the ______________________ in a

There are some rules to remember about the Sankey diagram:
(1) Each energy source and loss in the process is represented by an arrow.
(2) The diagram is drawn to scale with the width of an arrow being proportional to the
amount of energy it represents.
(3) The energy flow is drawn from left to right.
(4) When energy is lost from the system it moves to the top or bottom of the diagram.
(5) Power transfers as well as energy flows can be represented.
